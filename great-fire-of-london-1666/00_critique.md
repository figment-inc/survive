## LINE-LEVEL CRITIQUE

### You-As-Subject — 7/10

**Failing line (Clip 03):** "You watch thirteen thousand houses burn."
**Problem:** "You watch" is passive observation. The viewer is a spectator, not a participant suffering the fire. The strongest scripts make you ACT or SUFFER, not witness.
**Fix:** "You drag what you can carry toward the river. Thirteen thousand houses burn behind you."

### Escalation Curve — 7/10

**Problem:** The escalation is linear and predictable — fire starts, response fails, fire spreads. There's no TURN, no moment where the viewer thinks they understand how bad it is and then it gets worse in an unexpected direction. The mayor's refusal is the right beat but it's introduced as context in Clip 02 rather than landing as a twist after the viewer expects SOME response to work.
**Fix:** Consider restructuring so Clip 02 establishes the bucket effort (you're trying), Clip 03 introduces the mayor's refusal as the TURN (escape route closing — "the mayor could stop this by demolishing a row of houses. He refuses."), then Clip 04 delivers the catastrophe and revelation compressed. This gives the viewer a moment of "wait, someone CHOSE not to stop this?"

### Gut Punch — 6/10

**Failing line (Clip 03):** "Four days. The fire jumps block by block."
**Problem:** Factual and well-paced, but the viewer's body doesn't react. "Jumps block by block" is informational, not sensory. The script is mostly a well-told account rather than an experience. Only "molten lead pools at your feet" touches visceral territory.
**Fix:** Add one more sensory/physical beat earlier — e.g., in Clip 02: "You grab a bucket. The heat pushes you back at thirty paces." Or in Clip 03: "The smoke is so thick you navigate by sound — timber cracking, glass bursting."
