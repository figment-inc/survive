## LINE-LEVEL CRITIQUE

### You-As-Subject — 7/10

**Failing line (Clip 01):** "Germany's parliament building is burning — and the government already has a plan."
**Problem:** The subject is "Germany's parliament" and "the government" — the viewer is watching from a news desk, not living in Berlin. Two full sentences with no "you."
**Fix:** Keep the hook's clarity but thread "you" in earlier. Consider: "Germany's parliament building is burning — and the government already has a plan for you." One word shifts the target onto the viewer.

**Failing line (Clip 02):** "A lone arsonist sets fire to the Reichstag. He's caught inside."
**Problem:** Two sentences of third-person reporting. "He" is the subject, not "you." The viewer doesn't enter the story until "you don't get an investigation."
**Fix:** "A lone arsonist sets fire to the Reichstag. You watch the flames from your apartment window. But you don't get an investigation — you get an excuse." This also helps with the word count issue, so you'd need to trim elsewhere to compensate.

### Narrator Restraint — 7/10

**Failing line (Clip 01):** "and the government already has a plan"
**Problem:** This is soft foreshadowing. It tells the viewer "something was pre-arranged" before the story earns that revelation. The ending — "The decree was drafted before the ashes cooled" — is where that revelation belongs. Putting it in the hook steals the ending's punch.
**Fix:** Remove the foreshadow from the hook entirely. Let the fire be the hook: "Germany's parliament building is on fire. You would not want to be in Berlin, 1933." The pre-planning revelation hits harder if the viewer discovers it only at the end.
