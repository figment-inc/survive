## LINE-LEVEL CRITIQUE

### Escalation Curve — 7/10

**Failing line (Clip 04):** "Shells fall every hour. You can't sleep, can't surface, can't surrender — your commander refuses to negotiate."

**Problem:** The bombardment was already established in Clip 02 ("survive the shelling"), so Clip 04 intensifying to "shells fall every hour" is linear — more of the same, slightly worse. The commander's refusal to negotiate IS a turn-worthy beat (an escape route closing), but it arrives as a clause tacked onto the end of a list. It deserves to be the structural pivot of the clip, not a subordinate phrase. Right now the viewer experiences: bad → worse → even worse → worst. There's no moment where the *type* of threat changes or an unexpected door closes.

**Fix:** Make the commander's refusal the centerpiece of the clip, and frame it as a hope crushed — the viewer expects surrender is possible, then watches it get taken away:

*"By late June, white flags appear along your lines. Soldiers on both sides are ready to stop. Your commander orders the flags torn down — he refuses to negotiate."*

This creates a turn: the viewer thought escape was possible, then the authority figure slams the door. It shifts from environmental suffering to human-caused suffering, which re-engages attention.
