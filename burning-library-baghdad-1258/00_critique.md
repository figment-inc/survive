## LINE-LEVEL CRITIQUE

### Escalation Curve — 6/10

**Failing line (Clip 03):** "Thirteen days of catapult bombardment. The walls crack open. Cavalry floods the streets."

**Problem:** This is entirely predictable from Clip 02. The viewer already knows the walls aren't built for siege, so they crack — expected. There's no turn. No moment where the viewer thinks "wait, it gets WORSE?" The destruction of manuscripts is devastating but it's the same direction of bad, not a new dimension of bad.

**Fix:** Insert a turn — the caliph attempts to negotiate AFTER the siege begins, and the Mongols refuse. This creates a hope-crushed moment: "On day five, your caliph finally offers surrender. Hülegü refuses. He wants an example." Then the massacre hits differently because an exit closed.

---

### Educational Completeness — 6/10

**Failing line (Clip 02):** "Your caliph refused to surrender. Now a hundred and fifty thousand cavalry surround your walls."

**Problem:** The causal chain skips a link. WHY did the caliph refuse? Was it arrogance? Bad intelligence? A religious conviction? Without this, the viewer can't fully explain the disaster's root cause to a friend. Also: the transition from "walls crack" to total urban massacre needs one beat — did the garrison fight? Flee? The viewer can't explain how a city of hundreds of thousands fell in days.

**Fix:** Add one phrase of motivation: "Your caliph refused to surrender — he told the Mongols God would protect Baghdad." This makes the human decision legible and teachable. For the breach: "The garrison throws down weapons" or "Your army died on the walls" — one beat showing why there's no urban defense.
