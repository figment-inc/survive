## LINE-LEVEL CRITIQUE

### Educational Completeness — 6/10

**Failing line (Clip 01):** "Then five people are bitten in twelve days along the Jersey Shore."
**Problem:** The hook mentions five attacks but the script never explains the institutional response to the earlier attacks — did authorities dismiss them? Refuse to close beaches? The viewer can't explain to a friend WHY the creek attack was allowed to happen. The causal chain skips from "experts said it couldn't happen" to "it happened inland" without showing how expert denial enabled continued exposure.
**Fix:** Replace the shark's journey in Clip 02 with the human failure: "Experts call the first attacks impossible — a shark in New Jersey waters. Authorities refuse to close a single beach. You live eleven miles inland on a tidal creek. A great white follows the current upstream." (May need trimming elsewhere to stay under 90.)

### Understanding — 7/10

**Failing line (Clip 02):** "A juvenile great white follows the current upstream — somewhere every expert said it couldn't survive."
**Problem:** "Every expert said it couldn't survive" is stated but never grounded — the viewer doesn't understand what the experts believed or why they were wrong. This makes the disaster feel like pure bad luck rather than institutional failure. The human fingerprint (authorities refusing to act on early attacks) is absent from the causal chain.
**Fix:** One sentence establishing that officials dismissed early warnings or refused beach closures would elevate this from "freak event" to "preventable disaster." E.g., "Resort owners lobby to keep beaches open — tourism season can't stop for one shark."
